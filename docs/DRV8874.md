# DRV8874 OverCurrent Protection (OCP)

The DRV8874 has a built-in overcurrent protection, called **IOCP**, that triggers when the output current exceeds a fixed internal threshold. This threshold is not user-configurable and is specified as 6 A minimum, 10 A typical. If the output current exceeds **IOCP** for longer than 3 µs (*t*<sub>OCP</sub>), the H-bridge is disabled and the **nFAULT** pin is driven low.

What happens next depends on the **IMODE** setting. In automatic retry mode (Quad-Level 1 or 2), the outputs remain disabled for 2 ms (*t*<sub>RETRY</sub>), after which the H-bridge is re-enabled. If the overcurrent condition persists, the cycle repeats. In latched-off mode (Quad-Level 3 or 4), the outputs remain disabled until the device is reset via **nSLEEP** or by removing **VM**. Reset via **nSLEEP** also takes at least 2 ms (*t*<sub>SLEEP</sub> + *t*<sub>WAKE</sub>).

It is not possible to disable **IOCP** or prevent it from operating by, for example, configuring the current regulator (**ITRIP**) to a lower value. The reason is that the **ITRIP** current regulation loop responds far too slowly.

According to the DRV8874 datasheet, the response consists of several timing components: the current mirror delay, *t*<sub>DELAY</sub> (1.6 µs), the blanking time after a switching edge, *t*<sub>BLK</sub> (1.1 µs), and the comparator deglitch time, *t*<sub>DEG</sub> (0.6 µs). Together these add up to an absolute minimum response time of 3.3 µs.

In practice, the actual response time is considerably longer due to additional delays introduced by the internal comparator, the gate-driver logic, and any capacitance present on the **IPROPI** pin.

### DCC Output Stage Requirements

A DCC output stage drives a track that presents a highly capacitive load. Locomotives and wagons equipped with onboard capacitors must charge these capacitors when power is first applied. The resulting inrush currents are a normal part of operation and must not be interpreted as a fault condition.

The inrush current is governed by **I = C × dV/dt**. For example, a fully discharged 1000 µF capacitor connected to a 16 V DCC supply will draw an inrush current that is limited almost entirely by the parasitic resistance and inductance of the circuit. As a result, the current rises extremely rapidly and can easily exceed the **IOCP** threshold of 6–10 A within 3 µs, causing OCP to trigger.

When OCP triggers, the H-bridge is disabled for the retry interval (*t*<sub>RETRY</sub>) of 2 ms. However, during the preceding 3 µs the capacitor has accumulated only a negligible amount of charge. Consequently, when the H-bridge is re-enabled, the capacitor is still essentially discharged and generates the same inrush current again. OCP triggers once more, the H-bridge is disabled again, and the cycle repeats indefinitely. As a result, the capacitor never reaches its operating voltage and the DCC signal never becomes stable.

### Inrush Handling

There is no simple way to prevent OCP from triggering during capacitor inrush. DCC-EX, among others, solves this by using a dedicated PWM inrush sequence. The principle is to keep each ON pulse shorter than the OCP response time (approximately 3 µs), preventing OCP from triggering. The OFF time must be short enough to charge the track capacitors within a reasonable time, yet long enough to prevent the DRV8874 from entering thermal shutdown (TSD). DCC-EX uses an OFF time of 13 µs, although a longer OFF time may be desirable to reduce chip temperature.

The sequence of events is as follows:

1. The H-bridge starts operating normally.
2. If an OCP event occurs, **nFAULT** is asserted and the DRV8874 disables the H-bridge for the retry interval (*t*<sub>RETRY</sub>) of 2 ms. During this interval, **nFAULT** remains asserted.
3. Detection of **nFAULT** causes the software to enter **ALERT** mode. In this mode, the normal DCC waveform is replaced by the special PWM inrush sequence, preventing OCP from triggering again.
4. During the initial 2 ms retry interval the H-bridge remains disabled, so no output is generated. Once the retry interval expires, the PWM inrush sequence appears on the track, allowing the capacitors to charge gradually.
5. With **IMODE** configured for automatic retry, every PWM transition clears **nFAULT**. If the output current again exceeds the **IOCP** threshold, **nFAULT** is immediately asserted and the retry sequence repeats. Once the track capacitors are sufficiently charged, the current no longer exceeds the threshold, **nFAULT** remains inactive, and the software can leave **ALERT** mode. If **nFAULT** continues to assert, the condition should be treated as a genuine track short circuit.

### Distinguishing Inrush from a Short Circuit

The DRV8874 datasheet recommends placing a capacitor between the **IPROPI** pin and ground to filter transient signals, preventing the current regulation circuitry from responding prematurely. The capacitor value can be adjusted as required, although larger values increase the response time of the current regulation loop. The datasheet recommends a value of 10 nF, resulting in a time constant of approximately 15 µs when used with a 1.5 kΩ *R*<sub>IPROPI</SUB> resistor.

As illustrated in Figure 15 of the datasheet, monitoring the **IPROPI** voltage provides an effective method of distinguishing a temporary capacitor inrush from a permanent DCC short circuit. Because the **IPROPI** capacitor averages the current over multiple 3 µs / 13 µs PWM cycles, the resulting voltage reflects the average current rather than the individual current peaks. During capacitor charging, the average current gradually decreases until normal operation can resume. In contrast, a genuine short circuit keeps the average current high, allowing the software to reliably distinguish between the two conditions.

### Inrush Protection Implementation

The DRV8874 supports both **PH/EN mode** and **PWM mode** for implementing the inrush 3 µs pulse sequence. To avoid introducing a DC offset on the track, the 3 µs pulse must be applied symmetrically during both polarities of the DCC waveform.

In the DRV8874 **PH/EN mode**, the 3 µs pulse is generated by setting the **EN** input high while driving **PH** alternately driving PH high and low. During the 13 µs OFF period, **EN** must be low.

In the DRV8874 **PWM mode**, the 3 µs pulse is generated by driving either **IN1** or **IN2** high while keeping the other input low.

In **PWM mode** the 13 µs OFF period can be implemented using **coast** or **brake** mode. For designs without an output inductor, both modes are functionally suitable and the practical difference is generally small. When an output inductor is fitted, however, **brake** mode is generally preferred. In **coast** mode, the inductor current commutates through the DRV8874 body diodes, whereas **brake** mode provides a controlled freewheel path through the low-side MOSFETs.

### Output Inductor

One possible hardware solution to reduce the effect of capacitor inrush current is to place a power inductor between the H-bridge output and the track. The inductor limits the rate at which the output current can increase, making it less likely that the **IOCP** threshold is reached during the first few microseconds after power is applied. As an additional benefit, the inductor also reduces the output slew rate, lowering EMI and ringing on the DCC signal.

The required inductance follows directly from
**dI/dt = V/L** or **L = V × t / I**

For example, if the objective is to limit the current to 6 A after 3 µs, and the DCC output switches between +16 V and −16 V (32 V across the H-bridge), the required inductance is

**L = 32 V × 3 µs / 6 A = 16 µH**

In practice, such a large inductance noticeably degrades the DCC signal quality. Values between 4.7 µH and 10 µH are therefore a more practical compromise, providing some reduction of the inrush current while maintaining acceptable signal integrity.

### Current Measurement - ADC

Besides providing the programmable current regulation threshold (**ITRIP**), the **IPROPI** output can also be used to measure the  track current with an ADC. The **IPROPI** pin sources a current proportional to the H-bridge output current. Passing this current through the **R<sub>IPROPI</sub>** resistor produces a voltage that can be sampled by the microcontroller.

For the DRV8874, the relationship is: **V<sub>IPROPI</sub> = I<sub>OUT</sub> × R<sub>IPROPI</sub> × 450 µA/A**

With **R<sub>IPROPI</sub> = 2.49 kΩ** (the value of the  **R<sub>IPROPI</sub>** resistor on the Pololu / AliExpress modules), the output voltage becomes approximately **1.12 V/A**. An output current of 2.9 A produces an **IPROPI** voltage of approximately **3.25 V**.

### Summary

The DRV8874 can be used as output stage of a DCC command station or booster, but its fixed **IOCP** protection requires special consideration. Capacitive loads on the track can generate inrush currents that exceed the **IOCP** threshold within a few microseconds, causing repeated 2 ms shutdown cycles that prevent normal DCC operation.

Because **IOCP** cannot be disabled and the programmable current regulation (**ITRIP**) responds too slowly to prevent it, capacitor inrush must be handled in software. DCC-EX uses a dedicated 3 µs / 13 µs pulse sequence to charge the track capacitors gradually without triggering **IOCP**. Monitoring **IPROPI** and **nFAULT** enables the software to distinguish temporary capacitor charging from a genuine track short circuit. Although an output inductor can reduce the inrush current, excessive inductance degrades DCC signal quality and therefore represents a compromise rather than a complete solution.
